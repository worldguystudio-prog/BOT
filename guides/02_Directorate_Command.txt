===============================================================================
              ORGVNUM — DIRECTORATE COMMAND GUIDE
                       (Permission Level 80)
===============================================================================

You're senior command. Directorate sits above moderators and department heads,
below only administrators and the owner. Think of yourself as the command
staff — you set the tone, post the big announcements, and have oversight over
the whole operation.


WHAT YOU CAN DO:
───────────────

POST OFFICIAL ANNOUNCEMENTS (/announcement)
  Posts a branded announcement that pings @everyone by default.

    /announcement title:Weekly Brief body:New protocols in effect Monday.
    /announcement title:Promotions body:Congrats to our new mods! mention_everyone:false

  Use the mention_everyone:false option if you want to post without pinging
  the whole server. Only Directorate and above can use this.


EVERYTHING MODERATORS CAN DO:
  Read 03_Moderators.txt for the full list. Quick summary:
    /warn /mute /timeout /kick /ban /softban /purge /slowmode /lock /case
    /warnings /nickname

  Plus:
    /training create/start/end/cancel/roster
    /shift start/end/status/leaderboard
    /points user:@someone amount:50
    /dispatch /scene /alert /callsign

  Plus all ticket, application, and waitlist management commands.


WHAT YOU CAN'T DO:
─────────────────

  These require Administrator (90) or Owner (100):

  ❌ /dashboard — configure bot settings
  ❌ /config — same, via typed commands
  ❌ /ticket-panel, /application-panel — post system panels
  ❌ /automod enable/disable — toggle auto-moderation
  ❌ /lockdown /unlockdown — server-wide emergency lockdown
  ❌ /clearwarnings — wipe someone's entire warning record
  ❌ /reload /sync /database /debug — owner-only maintenance


YOUR ROLE IN PRACTICE:
─────────────────────

  • You're the public face of leadership. Use /announcement for the
    big stuff — policy changes, promotions, events, server-wide notices.

  • You have full moderation power. If a situation escalates beyond
    what a regular moderator should handle, you step in.

  • You can create and run training sessions, events, and operations.
    Read 05_Trainers.txt for details.

  • You can review applications and run the waitlist. Read
    04_Recruiters.txt for details.

  • You manage shifts and can see the leaderboard. Read
    09_Shifts_and_Activity.txt.


TIPS:
────

  • Don't overuse /announcement with @everyone. People mute the server
    if it pings too often. Save it for the important stuff.

  • If you need to make a quick broadcast that's less formal, use
    /alert instead (pings @here, not @everyone).

  • When you moderate someone, they get a DM automatically. You don't
    need to message them separately.

  • Every action you take is logged to the mod log channel. Be
    deliberate — your name's on the record.


THE COMMAND YOU'LL USE MOST:
────────────────────────────

  /announcement title:... body:...

That's your signature move. Use it well.
